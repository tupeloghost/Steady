I n early August, a freight truck pulled up to an OhioHealth facility in Columbus and took roughly 30,000 pounds of N95 masks and hot-and-cold packs, all boxed, sealed and stamped with expiration dates still a year or two out. Left alone, the estimated $250,000 worth of medical supplies would eventually have been thrown away, deemed unlikely to be required before their use-by date. Reasons to be Cheerful

The operation was part of Project MedBridge, a medical redistribution initiative set up by 16-year-old Ben Kurian, a junior at Olentangy Liberty High School in Powell, Ohio. Reasons to be Cheerful Over and over he heard the same uncomfortable fact, every year, big hospitals like OhioHealth and Cardinal Health discard thousands of pounds of good quality supplies simply because they won ' t be used before their expiration date passes, a problem greatly exacerbated by the pandemic-driven overstock. How this works, and which outlets are included

These are inputs to compare, not sources to trust. Outlets are carried across the political spectrum, including state broadcasters, precisely so that a story can be read against itself. Every outlet is labelled by lean, owner and money. Agreement across outlets that disagree is the only thing treated as evidence.

Each morning Steady reads news outlets from the left, center and right, plus government-funded and independent ones. Stories that several of them covered go first.

The quotes under each story are things outlets on different sides both reported, in their own words.

Headlines are reworded to remove charged language. Tap "See every outlet's headline" to read the originals.
