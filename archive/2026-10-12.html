BY THE OPTIMIST Daily EDITORIAL Team On Saturday, September 22, solar and wind met 80.4 percent of demand on Australia's national electricity market, with the grid covering the eastern states, South Australia, and the Australian Capital Territory. The Optimist Daily

Reported by The Optimist Daily

MoreLess

For about 30 minutes around lunchtime, rooftop panels on homes and businesses across the country carried more than half of the total load by themselves. The Optimist Daily Large-scale solar and wind farms covered most of the rest, with hydro adding around one percent. The Optimist Daily Rooftop solar as a grid-scale contributor More than 4.3 million Australian households now have rooftop solar installed. The Optimist Daily

Saturday's record showed what those installations add up to in practice: enough distributed generation to carry the country through the middle of the day without needing much from the main grid at all. The Optimist Daily Energy Minister Chris Bowen called it "more clean, cheap, sovereign energy powering our homes and businesses than ever before," and said more records should be expected as the transition continues. The Optimist Daily Why these records will keep falling Dr. Dylan McConnel l, an energy systems researcher at the University of New South Wales, called it "record season" for renewables. How this works, and which outlets are included

These are inputs to compare, not sources to trust. Outlets are carried across the political spectrum, including state broadcasters, precisely so that a story can be read against itself. Every outlet is labelled by lean, owner and money. Agreement across outlets that disagree is the only thing treated as evidence.

Each morning Steady reads news outlets from the left, center and right, plus government-funded and independent ones. Stories that several of them covered go first.

The quotes under each story are things outlets on different sides both reported, in their own words.

Headlines are reworded to remove charged language. Tap "See every outlet's headline" to read the originals.
